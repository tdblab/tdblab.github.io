FISHER_α

Flexible Instrument for Stimulus-controlled Habituation and Experimental Reinforcement

An automatic fish feeder that pairs every feeding with a light cue. It lets researchers study how fish learn, form habits and respond to drugs, with the same timing every time, day after day.

Why we built it

Learning experiments in fish need the same thing to happen at the same time, over and over, for days or weeks. Doing that by hand is slow, tiring and easy to get slightly wrong. FISHER_α sits on top of the tank and does it automatically: it shows a light, delivers the reward, and keeps count.

Each unit is built around an Arduino in a 3D-printed case, so it is small, low cost and easy to repeat across many tanks.

How one trial works

  1. 1

    Light cue

    A red or green LED turns on above the tank. The fish learn to link each color with what comes next.

  2. 2

    Countdown

    The OLED screen shows which trial is running and counts down the time left.

  3. 3

    Red trial: food

    A servo swings a flap under the food pod, and pellets drop into the tank through a hole in the floor.

  4. 4

    Green trial: air pump

    A small air pump runs for 2 seconds while the green light stays on.

  5. 5

    Trial done

    The light turns off, the screen goes back home, and the trial count goes up by one.

Features

Built to run on its own

Two rechargeable 21700 batteries power each unit, so it can feed on schedule without anyone in the room.

Keeps time through power loss

A real-time clock with its own coin-cell backup keeps the schedule correct even if the main power is switched off.

Simple controls

Four buttons and a small OLED screen set the schedule. A battery gauge shows charge at a glance.

Light sensors

Two light sensors (LDRs) can read the light around the tank for experiments that need it.

Scales to many tanks

Units are small and identical, so a whole rack of tanks can run the same experiment side by side.

Coming next: FISHER EYE

A version with a built-in camera that looks down into the tank and records a short clip every few minutes, saved by date and time, so behavior can be checked over months.
