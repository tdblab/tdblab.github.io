About Me

My work sits where molecular, evolutionary and developmental biology meet machine learning. I want to understand how new body features develop, how tissues get their patterns, and how nerve circuits are wired.

Wet lab and engineering

What defines my research is that I work in both high-end wet-lab biology and engineering. I keep large colonies of model organisms and I am skilled in:

  • Molecular and genomic: CRISPR-Cas9 editing, single-cell and bulk RNA sequencing, qPCR and tissue culture.
  • Imaging and spatial: multiplexed fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH), antibody staining and laser capture microdissection.
  • Engineering: new tools to detect mRNA and protein, farming technology, object detection, robotics and computer vision, both hardware and software.

Scientific contribution

Over more than 9 years of research I have published in peer-reviewed journals including Science Advances, Science, Nature Ecology and Evolution, Communications Biology, Journal of Neuroscience, Development, Developmental Biology Advances, Scientific Reports, iScience, Cell Reports, Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution, Methods and Protocols, Genomics and Bioresource Technology.

I also review papers for eLife, BMC Biology, BMC Research Notes, Annals of the Entomological Society of America, PLoS Genetics, Journal of Insect Physiology, Biology, Cells, Molecules and Insect Sciences.

Mentoring and teaching

Mentoring is a core part of my career. I have mentored 4 graduate, 13 undergraduate and 3 high school students at NUS, and I taught and assisted classes in evolutionary developmental biology (Evo-Devo).
